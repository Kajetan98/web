Prototype
Wearable / Emergency record

A patient record a paramedic opens with a single tap.

MediTag is a waterproof NFC band. HERO — Health Emergency Read Out — is the platform where a patient and their doctor keep one record: allergies, current medication, chronic conditions, blood group, implants and emergency contacts. The emergency read-out needs no login, including when the patient is unconscious.

How it works / 01

The band carries an address, not data

The NFC tag holds no medical information at all — only the address of the record. A lost band is therefore not a leaked medical file but a lost identifier, one the platform can revoke.

The paramedic installs nothing and signs in nowhere. Tapping the phone opens the record, where the critical set is ordered by what matters at the scene rather than by the order the form was filled in.

Knowing whose word it is

Every entry carries its source. A patient's entry and a doctor's entry look different because they mean different things: "the patient says so" is not the same premise as "a doctor confirmed it". A doctor's entry also shows which doctor signed it and under which licence number — the server stamps that signature on save, so it cannot be forged from the browser or stripped afterwards.

Roles / 02

One record, three different levels of access.

Who sees what, and what they authenticate with. The PIN guards editing, not rescue — that distinction shapes the whole permission model.

Patient

Creates the record, sets the PIN, keeps every section and sees the read history. Deletes the record and its history with one button.

Band identifier + PIN

Doctor

Registers an account with their medical licence number, opens the record with the patient's code and fills in diagnoses, medication and allergies. Every entry carries their name and licence number, and the visit is logged.

Licence account + band identifier + patient's code

Paramedic

Taps the phone and gets the critical set — no PIN, no account, no install. The read-out is logged together with the reader's description.

Band identifier alone

Emergency read-out / 03

Ordered for the first few minutes.

The paramedic's screen is not a table of contents. It is a list of decisions, in the order they are taken at the scene.

  1. 01

    Allergies and anaphylaxis

    Right at the top, with anaphylactic reactions flagged separately. This is what changes a decision to give a drug fastest.

  2. 02

    Medication, anticoagulants highlighted

    Anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs are marked separately, because they change how trauma and bleeding are handled.

  3. 03

    Active conditions

    Diagnoses with an ICD-10 code and a status. Past conditions stay in the patient's record but do not clutter the emergency screen.

  4. 04

    Implants and notes for the crew

    Pacemaker, pump, mechanical valve, difficult IV access, a declared DNR, consent to organ donation.

  5. 05

    Emergency contact and the audit trail

    The first-choice contact number ready to dial, and an entry in the record's history: when it was opened and by whom.

Data / 04

Where the data sits, and what the server never holds

The PIN never leaves the browser. The client derives a digest from it, and only that reaches the server, where it goes through scrypt with a random salt. A database leak therefore reveals neither the PIN nor the value the browser sends.

The public set — the same one the paramedic sees — carries neither the PIN nor the read history. The history opens only against the PIN, for the patient and for a doctor with access.

With no server running, the application works entirely in the browser and keeps records in its local storage. That is the mode the prototype linked above runs in.

Deliberately out of scope

  • The emergency read-out is open to anyone who knows the band identifier — in production that identifier must be long and random
  • There are no doctor accounts and no medical-licence verification; a doctor enters with the patient's code
  • There is no PIN attempt limit and no TLS layer on the server itself
  • Health data is a special category of personal data — a deployment needs an impact assessment and encryption at rest

HERO is a prototype. It is not a medical device, it has not been clinically validated, and it replaces neither the emergency interview nor medical records. The prototype on this site stores data only in the browser — do not enter real patient data into it.
