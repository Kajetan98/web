About / 01

Technology with a human centre of gravity.

We don't build hardware meant to impress on a spec sheet. We build hardware you can trust when the nearest hospital is out of reach.

Why we exist

A century of medicine has been designed around the assumption that patient and physician are in the same place. Exploration breaks that assumption.

In orbit, at a polar station or on a deep-sea expedition, a consultation may be delayed by minutes or impossible for hours. Medical equipment that performs well on a hospital ward loses its foundations out there: stable power, calibration for Earth's gravity, a continuous link, and a doctor watching the screen.

SPACER exists to close that gap from the engineering and the clinical side at once. We design systems that gather trustworthy data when nobody is supervising them, and present it in a form someone can act on — including when that someone is a crew member with no medical training.

A good field medical device is one that says plainly what it does not know.

That is why we spend so much attention on how the system behaves at the edges: a lost sensor signal, an uncertain reading, a dropped link. Those moments decide whether the hardware is trusted — not accuracy under ideal conditions.

Principles

Four rules we don't bend.

01

Offline is the default state

We don't treat a lost link as a failure. The system runs fully on site and forwards data once a transmission window opens.

02

Data supports the physician, never replaces them

The algorithm organises observations and flags priorities. Diagnosis and treatment decisions stay with a person.

03

Every gram and every watt must be justified

Mass and power budgets on a mission are fixed. A feature that doesn't change a medical decision doesn't earn a place on board.

04

Every decision can be reconstructed

A full record of what the system measured and recommended stays available for post-mission analysis. Without it nothing can be improved or accounted for.

What we don't do

The scope we deliberately leave out says as much about the company as a list of capabilities:

  • We don't diagnose automatically — the system describes deviations, it doesn't name a disease.
  • We don't collect data “just in case”. Every measured parameter needs a clinical reason.
  • We don't promise performance outside the range we have actually validated.
  • We don't replace our partners' emergency procedures — we fit into them.
